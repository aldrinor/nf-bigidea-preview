A charge that fades under smoke

Federal agencies tell Americans to fight wildfire smoke with a MERV-13 filter — but that rating is measured on a clean, brand-new filter. Much of its filtering comes from a built-in electrostatic charge, and smoke strips that charge as it builds up. In an EPA study, a MERV-13 air cleaner lost about 95% of its cleaning power after just 10 milligrams of smoke — about an hour's worth. The airflow barely changes, so nothing warns you it has stopped working.
