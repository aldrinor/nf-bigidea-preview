The problem

A charge that fades under smoke

Most high-grade air filters work by holding a static electric charge that pulls particles in. Wildfire smoke quietly kills it. In an EPA study, a charged filter lost about 95% of its cleaning power within a few hours of smoke — while the airflow barely changed, so nothing tells you it has stopped working.

Holder et al., EPA, Indoor Air 2022 ↗
The question isn't whether a filter starts with a charge — it's whether the charge survives the smoke.
