An ordinary filter catches mold spores — but it does not kill them. In the warm, damp media, the spores stay alive, settle in, and grow.

The University of Cincinnati measured a loaded filter shedding up to 12% of the mold spores it caught back into the air — about 60 times the rate of plain dust.

The filter becomes a source of the very mold it was meant to remove — and the more it collects, the more it can breed and release.

The Technical University of Denmark measured office staff about 10% slower behind a used filter than a clean one — a loaded filter also drags down the whole room it feeds.
