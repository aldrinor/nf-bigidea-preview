Wildfire Smoke

What it is, where it is, and why the recommended filter can't stop it.

Scroll to begin
01A charged particle

Wildfire Smoke is mostly soot — fine and ultrafine particles, most under 2.5 micrometers wide. Most of them carry an electric charge,1 and the air filters built to catch them carry a charge too.

02Scale

One large fire releases these particles by the trillion, and the wind carries them hundreds to thousands of miles. Smoke from fires in the West routinely reaches the East Coast and Canada.

03Current extent

Wildfire Smoke is no longer a regional problem. In recent years, an estimated 95 to 100 percent of the lower-48 population breathed it at some point each year.3

And the death toll is climbing. Fine-particle pollution from smoke is estimated to cause about 41,000 premature U.S. deaths a year (2011–2020 average), rising toward 71,000 by 2050 on a high-warming path — some 1.9 million lives between 2026 and 2055.4

04Deposition and health

The most dangerous particles are the smallest. A PM2.5 particle is about one-thirtieth the width of a human hair5 — small enough to lodge deep in the lungs, and for some to cross into the bloodstream.6

05Filter performance under smoke

Federal guidance recommends a MERV-13 or higher filter during smoke events.11 Many such filters are electret media: they hold a manufactured static charge that adds to mechanical filtration.

In EPA testing, a charged filter's clean-air delivery rate fell about 95 percent after roughly 10 milligrams of smoke had deposited — a few hours of a moderate event — while airflow resistance rose only about 7 percent.12 Because the pressure change is small, the loss of performance produces no clog and no usual indication that the filter is spent. The charge can be restored only by washing the filter in alcohol, which is not done in practice.

Filter ratings reflect this. A filter can be rated as MERV, measured with its charge intact, and as MERV-A, measured after the electrostatic charge is neutralized (ASHRAE 52.2, Appendix J) — the second being closer to performance under real loading.13 A filter that depends on a manufactured charge earns a high MERV rating and a markedly lower MERV-A.

So the real question for any filter is not whether it starts with a charge — but whether that charge survives the smoke.
