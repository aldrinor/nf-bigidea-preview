Mold survives almost anything —and it's negatively charged

Mold is one of the most common indoor allergens — and one of the hardest to get rid of. Its spores shrug off heat, drying, even bleach, then grow back wherever they land. About half of U.S. homes have a mold or dampness problem, and the EPA warns that breathing it can trigger allergic reactions and asthma attacks. Researchers estimate mold sets off 4.6 million asthma cases a year. It hits home value, too: mold is a defect sellers must disclose, and cleanup routinely runs into the thousands. And every spore carries a negative charge.
