An ordinary filter traps mold spores, but they stay alive in the media — that's how it becomes a source. NanoFlashing is engineered with a lasting positive charge, so mold spores it captures die on contact.

Tested with live airborne Aspergillus spores — not spores wetted first in a dish, the way most filter tests work — at Massachusetts General Hospital and Harvard Medical School, NIH-funded. The study has not yet been peer-reviewed or certified.
