The study behind NanoFlashing Fig. 1  ·  spore viability by filter  ·  wind tunnel, breathing speed

Mansour Lab · Massachusetts General Hospital & Harvard Medical School
Wind-tunnel study · submitted for peer review, not yet certified

An ordinary filter traps mold and leaves it alive. A positively charged filter killed almost all it caught.

Of the live spores the charged filter caught, just 0.76% were still alive afterward — about 8 in every 1,000. Run the same test on an ordinary, uncharged filter and the mold it trapped stayed alive.

01

Aerosolize

Live Aspergillus spores are released dry, the way they actually travel in air — not wetted first, as most filter tests do.

02

Draw through

The spore-laden air passes through the filter at 0.97 m/s — about the speed of a quiet breath.

03

Culture the catch

The captured spores are cultured to count how many survive. A second, uncharged filter is run identically as the control.
